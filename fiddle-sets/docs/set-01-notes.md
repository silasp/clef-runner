# Set 1 — *Hector's Crossing*: arranging notes

**Route:** Scotland → Denmark → Ireland → Scotland/Cape Breton.
**Shape:** free slow air → bouncy dance → lilting minor-mode slip jig → driving
reel → a short coda that quotes the opening tune. About 7 minutes.
Rehearsal letters below match the score.

| Tune | Meter | Key | Tempo | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hector the Hero | 3/4 | A | ♩ = 54, freely | 1 |
| Randers Hopsa | 2/4 | D (B part in A) | ♩ = 104 | 3 |
| A Fig for a Kiss | 9/8 | E dorian | ♩. = 104 | 3–4 |
| Miss McLeod's | 4/4 | A | ♩ = 176 | 5 |

## 1. Hector the Hero (A–D)
* **A** — Violin I alone for four bars, then Violin II slides in with a low open-string
  drone (E–A, D–A, B–E) that moves with the harmony.
* **B** — A counter-melody of long guide tones. Where the tune holds a long
  note, Violin II echoes the tune's triplet turn underneath it.
* **C** — The B part in full 3rds and 6ths, swelling up to the high A and easing off.
  Reharmonised with Bm7 and C♯m–F♯m.
* **D** — Harmony, then guide tones (F♯–E, D–C♯) under a *rit.* to a fermata.

**Transition 1 (2 bars, 2/4).** Violin II sets the new tempo alone with an
oom-pah on A7 (the dominant of D). Violin I then turns Hector's rising pickup into a
semiquaver run, A–B–C♯–D–E, that leads straight into the hopsa's pickup.

## 2. Randers Hopsa (E–J)
* **E** — Polka vamp: bass on the beat and short double-stops on the off-beats
  (A + F♯ on D chords, C♯ + E on A chords).
* **F** — A Scandinavian-style second part (*andrestemme*): the arpeggios in
  3rds and 6ths. Where the tune drops to low A, the line hops above it for a note.
* **G** — The B part, now in A major: sustained guide tones with a scale fill into the repeat.
* **H** — Harmony in 3rds, with a crescendo to the stamping cadence.
* **I** — **Role swap.** Violin II plays the tune and Violin I plays a descant above it.
* **J** — Back to the tune with harmony underneath, *f*.

**Transition 2 (1 bar, 9/8, ♩ = ♩.).** The beat stays the same speed but is now split
into three instead of two. The hopsa's falling C-part line becomes broken 3rds
(F♯–D, E–C♯, D–B), which is exactly the shape of the slip jig's hook. It runs over
D – A – Bm, and Bm is the v chord of E dorian, so it leads into the new key.

## 3. A Fig for a Kiss (K–R), played twice
* **K** — A pipes-style drone pulsing in dotted crotchets on the low strings.
* **L** — Harmony under the tune.
* **M** — A walking counter-line in dotted crotchets: E–D–C♯–B descents.
* **N** — Harmony.
* **O** — Slip-jig vamp. Violin I drops to *mp* here.
* **P** — Both violins in unison, *f*.
* **Q** — Descant: Violin II plays a 3rd above the tune (up to B on the E string).
* **R** — Harmony under the tune, *f*, ending on E.

**Transition 3 (2 bars, 4/4).** Violin II kicks off the reel groove alone on E7.
Violin I then plays the slip jig's cadence figure (B C♯ D B A G F E) in straight
quavers, with G♮ raised to G♯, which turns it into the dominant of A.

## 4. Miss McLeod's (S–Z), played twice
* **S** — Reel vamp (bass and off-beat chords).
* **T** — Octave doubling: Violin II plays the tune an octave lower.
* **U** — A walking guide-tone line in crotchets.
* **V** — Full harmony in 3rds and 6ths.
* **W** — Breakdown at *mp*: long notes under the tune.
* **X** — Harmony with a long crescendo.
* **Y** — A driving off-beat vamp, *f*.
* **Z** — Harmony, *ff*.

**Coda (2 bars).** Hector's opening phrase (C♯ – B C♯ B – A), *molto rit.*, ending on
a ringing A chord. Violin I plays an octave on the open A. Violin II plays a triple
stop, A–E–C♯.

## Things to listen for when refining
* B (Hector): the counter-melody sits low because the tune keeps dipping to A4.
  A descant above, or a hand-written line, are alternatives.
* M and U: the walking lines are generated. Freezing them from `parts.abc.txt`
  and adding some rhythm (a quaver pickup into each phrase) would make them more
  idiomatic.
* Tempos are suggestions. The reel at ♩ = 176 is a session tempo; start at about 140.
